01 / Research

and well-being.

Understanding the economic conditions that shape healthier lives.

Job market paper

Fresh Produce Prices and Mental Health

Under review · Journal of Health Economics

I estimate the causal effect of local fresh-produce price increases on population mental health in the United States. I combine county-level retail scanner data with repeated cross-sections from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), using weather-driven supply shocks in major growing regions interacted with distance-based exposure measures as an instrument.

Higher produce prices worsen population mental health, with larger effects in counties with limited baseline food access.

Work in progress

Early-stage projects on revealed food access and retail demand.

01

Revealed Prime Food-Retail Locations: A Mobility-Based Measure of Local Food Demand

Using Dewey Data to identify high-visit food-retail centers in Lexington/Fayette County and Kentucky, with sensitivity analyses across spatial scales, peak-separation rules, and anchor-versus-cluster retail typologies.

02

Demand Sheds, Leakage, and Bypass in Food Retail Markets

Linking prime food-retail destinations to home-origin mobility flows to map revealed demand sheds, quantify regional pull, and identify communities that bypass nearby retailers for larger shopping centers.

03

Nutrition Layers in Revealed Food Access: Product Availability, Prices, and Healthy Food Environments

Integrating retail mobility, product availability, prices, nutrition attributes, food-assistance context, and socio-demographic and health layers to evaluate the nutrition quality of revealed food-demand environments.
