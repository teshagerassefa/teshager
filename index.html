On The Faculty Market

Research at the intersection of food systems, policy, and well-being.

I am a faculty candidate in agricultural and applied economics with research interests in health economics, applied microeconomics, public policy, and food policy. My current work connects retail food prices and nutrition benefit design to mental health outcomes using large administrative and survey datasets.

Before joining the University of Kentucky, I taught statistics, economics, and policy analysis at Bahir Dar University and the University of Gondar, and coordinated research teams focused on small-scale irrigation and household welfare.

Job Market Paper

Fresh Produce Prices and Mental Health

How local produce price shocks affect population mental health in the United States.

This paper estimates the causal effect of local fresh-produce price increases on population mental health. Using county-level retail scanner data and repeated cross-sections from BRFSS, I instrument produce prices with weather-driven supply shocks in major growing regions interacted with distance-based exposure measures. Higher produce prices significantly worsen population mental health, with larger effects in counties with limited baseline food access.
